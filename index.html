SuperOver: a cricket game you play with your ears

SuperOver is an Android cricket game built for blind and visually impaired players. It works offline, speaks everything it does, and is played with simple swipes.

About the game

Most cricket games ask you to watch the screen. SuperOver does not. Every menu is spoken, every ball is described by a commentator, and every shot and delivery is chosen by gesture, so you can play a full match without seeing anything.

What you can do

  • Play a Quick Match in T20, ODI and other overs formats.
  • Bat, bowl and field using swipe gestures, with a gesture guide inside the game.
  • Pick your team, playing eleven, wicketkeeper and bowler each over.
  • Hear Hindi commentary, crowd noise, and the sound of bat, ball and stumps.
  • Save a match and resume it later.
  • Use the game with TalkBack on, or with TalkBack off and the game’s own spoken menus.

Good to know

  • No account, no login, no ads at the moment.
  • Fully offline. The game collects no personal data.
  • For Android 7 and above.
  • It is still being tested with a group of blind players before the Google Play launch planned for 1 January 2027.

What’s New

Version 11.3

  • A short match summary is read out after each match.
  • Extra commentary and a stadium drum effect for fifties, hundreds and big partnerships.
  • Batting now uses a beep as the cue, followed by a 3 second window to play your shot.
  • Stability fixes based on tester reports.

Version 11.0

  • New batting controls: think, then choose Attack, Big hit, Defend or Leave. No timing pressure.
  • Reworked ball and batting physics, so batsmen no longer get out at random.
  • Updated to meet current Android requirements.

Next up: more bug fixes, more deliveries such as the short ball and full toss, and practice modes.
